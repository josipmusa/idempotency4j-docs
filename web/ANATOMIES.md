# Anatomies

The proven structure per site type, checked against real sites and conversion guides (sources at the end). The agent picks one in the interview (Stage 1, round 2) and follows it; every table below is the default homepage order. Reordering or dropping a section is allowed with one line in `DECISIONS.md` saying why. Adding a section with no real content behind it is not allowed. Rows marked *optional* are included only when the interview gives a reason.

Every anatomy obeys the same rules:

- **Navbar:** logo, four to six links, one call-to-action button that stays visible. Login or account links are text, not a second button. Labels in the words the customer uses.
- **Hero:** answers what is this, who is it for, what do I do next, in under five seconds. Headline is the promise, not the name. One primary action; a secondary only if it is a different intent (call vs. write, buy vs. learn).
- **Proof within two screens.** Something that says "others trust this" appears right after or inside the hero: logos, a rating with a count, a named quote.
- **One action per page.** Every page ends in the site's one action or hands off to the page that does.
- **Footer:** contact and legal, address and hours where relevant (identical on every page), one repeated action. Never a second sitemap.
- **Special-moment slots** are marked ★ and are the only places moments live (`MOMENTS.md`).

## 1. SaaS / software product

Software people log into. Goal: trial, demo or sign-up.

What real sites do (Linear, Stripe, Notion): a one-sentence hero with the primary CTA and a "request demo" or "contact sales" secondary; social proof immediately after the hero (logos, "trusted by 98% of…", "powers 40,000 teams"); product shown as real UI, not as a video; three feature pillars with UI crops; testimonials near the bottom with name, role, company; a final CTA band; pricing only as a nav link; no FAQ on the homepage at the top tier.

| # | Section | What it does | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Navbar | Product, Pricing, Customers/Resources, Docs if relevant + sticky CTA | Log in as text; one CTA button |
| 2 | Hero ★ | Headline = the USP in benefit language; one line saying what it does; primary CTA ("Start free", "Get started"); secondary "Request a demo" only if sales exists | No clever vagueness; product name is not the headline |
| 3 | Social proof strip | 5–8 real logos, or "used by N teams", or a rating + count | No logos → one strong named quote; never invented |
| 4 | Product proof ★ | The product itself: real UI, scroll-driven walkthrough or interactive; a 2–3 minute video only as an alternative for small teams | Never a stock illustration of a dashboard |
| 5 | Feature pillars | Three blocks: benefit headline, two lines, a real UI crop each | "Features tell, benefits sell" |
| 6 | How it works ★ | 3–5 numbered steps with one visual, or a use-case gallery | Pinned scroll-stepped moment fits here |
| 7 | Testimonials | Three quotes with name, role, company, face; case-study link if any | |
| 8 | What's new *(optional)* | Changelog or three latest updates | Only for products that ship visibly |
| 9 | Pricing teaser *(optional)* | One line + link to Pricing; plans on the homepage only for very small products | Pricing lives on its own page |
| 10 | FAQs *(optional)* | 5–7 plain answers to the objections sales hears | Useful below the top tier |
| 11 | Final CTA | High-contrast band, one sentence, the primary action again | |
| 12 | Footer | Product, company, legal, social; newsletter only if they send one | |

**Sub-pages (default):** Pricing · Features (one, or one per key feature if the product is deep) · Customers or Case studies · About · Contact / Request a demo · Docs link · Changelog or Blog (only if they publish) · Privacy · Terms.

**Moments that fit:** scroll walkthrough of the real UI in slot 4; pinned steps in slot 6; dot-grid + spotlight or slow gradient mesh background. Cursor moments rarely.

## 2. Local business / service

Trades and services delivered at a place or at your home: carpenter, mechanic, electrician, clinic, salon. Goal: a call or a quote request.

What the evidence says: the phone number is the largest tappable thing above the fold on mobile, bigger than the logo and the button (a 24-pt tap-to-call outconverts a 14-pt one by about 2x); the hero carries service + city + rating with count + real photo; a trust strip follows; a services grid of 6–8 in customer language; the service area sits right after services, not in the footer; 6–9 real work photos with before/after pairs; 3–4 named static reviews; a 3–4 step process; starting prices or an FAQ; a contact block with at most four fields; a sticky "Call / Get a quote" bar on mobile once the hero scrolls away.

| # | Section | What it does | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Navbar | Links + phone number + CTA ("Get a quote") | Mobile: phone is the biggest tap target; sticky Call / Quote bar after the hero |
| 2 | Hero ★ | Service + city in the headline; rating with count; real photo of their work or team; primary CTA; phone | Emergency trades: two CTAs, "Emergency" and "Book" |
| 3 | Trust strip | Years, licence/certifications, insured, review score, brands worked with | Facts from the never-invent list only |
| 4 | Services | 6–8 cards in customer words, each links to its page | Fewer real services → fewer cards, never padding |
| 5 | Service area | Map or plain list of towns and neighbourhoods | Right here, because it answers "do you come to me" |
| 6 | Work / proof ★ | 6–9 real photos, before/after pairs, local captions | Best photos, not all of them |
| 7 | Reviews | 3–4 named reviews as static text (first name, initial, town, service) | Link to the source (Google) |
| 8 | Process | 3–4 numbered steps: contact → visit/quote → work → done | Reduces the fear of calling |
| 9 | Pricing / FAQs | Starting prices or flat rates where possible; otherwise 5–7 answers on price, timing, guarantee | Price ranges beat silence |
| 10 | Financing *(optional)* | One line for high-ticket trades | Only if offered |
| 11 | Contact block | Phone, hours, a form with at most four fields (name, phone, postcode, short description) | This is the one action |
| 12 | Footer | Name, address, phone, hours, legal; identical on every page | Matches the Google Business Profile exactly |

**Sub-pages (default):** one page per service (same shape: what, for whom, photos, price range, FAQ, CTA) · Work / Gallery · About (real team photos, two paragraphs) · Contact (form, map, hours, parking) · service-area pages only if they serve several towns and want local search · Privacy / Impressum as required.

**Moments that fit:** image curtain or mask reveal in the hero; before/after slider in slot 6; gallery hover or image trail on desktop; kinetic headline when the photography is weak; grain or paper background, warm. 3D only if the trade makes objects.

## 3. Product

One physical or digital product, or a small line. Goal: buy, pre-order, or find a retailer.

What real pages do (Apple, Teenage Engineering, DTC anatomy guides): hero with the product large, the name and a Buy button, with a sticky buy bar from the top; a highlights row (Apple: six scroll-animated cards); two to four feature chapters, each with its own visual, one of them an interactive 3D viewer; specs as a clean table; comparison against siblings or older models; accessories and the wider system as cross-sell; FAQ; reviews as the last conversion element on DTC pages; premium brands keep the price in the buy bar rather than the hero, DTC brands put price and a rating snippet in the hero.

| # | Section | What it does | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Navbar + sticky buy bar | Product, Specs, Support + "Buy" that stays visible from the top | Mobile: sticky buy bar with price |
| 2 | Hero ★ | The product large, one-line promise, primary CTA; price and rating snippet in the hero for DTC, in the buy bar for premium | Copy stays out of the product's way |
| 3 | Highlights | 3–6 short cards, one line each, the reasons to care | Scroll-driven reveal fits |
| 4 | Feature chapters ★ | 2–4 chapters, each a headline + two lines + its own visual; the first chapter is the signature: 3D viewer, turntable, exploded view | Benefit first, spec second |
| 5 | Details / specs | Materials, dimensions, what is in the box, compatibility | A clean table |
| 6 | In use | Lifestyle imagery or short clips of real people using it | Or none; never stock |
| 7 | Compare | Against siblings in the line or the previous model | Honest columns |
| 8 | Accessories / system *(optional)* | Cross-sell of what belongs with it | Only if it exists |
| 9 | FAQs | Shipping, returns, warranty, compatibility | Before reviews: it clears doubt |
| 10 | Reviews | Real reviews or press quotes | The last push |
| 11 | Buy | Price, variants, shipping, guarantee, the CTA | Repeats the sticky bar |
| 12 | Footer | Support, legal, socials | |

**Sub-pages (default):** Specs · Support / Manual / Downloads · Story / About · Where to buy or Shop · Compare (if a line) · Contact · Privacy · Terms / Warranty.

**Moments that fit:** 3D hero (model, or `img2threejs` from one clean photo) with cursor tilt on desktop and slow auto-rotate on mobile; scroll-scrubbed turntable or exploded view in the first chapter; spotlight background following the cursor; dark, product-first palettes.

## 4. Studio / portfolio

Architecture studio, photographer, designer, small agency whose past work is the argument. Goal: an inquiry.

What real studios do (BIG, Snøhetta, Pentagram): the homepage is the work. BIG is a project grid and a footer. Pentagram opens with a featured carousel, a one-line statement with discipline filters, then grids of eight projects per discipline, a partner quote between them, news, contact. Snøhetta: disciplines, a one-sentence statement, seven featured projects, news, a highlighted grid, contact. Nobody puts an about text on the homepage beyond one sentence; contact lives in the footer or one CTA. Project presentation is image + title + one line. Portfolio guides agree: 4–8 curated projects, not everything; project pages open on a hero image, give title and context (location, year, client, size), a concept statement under 100 words, drawings or process, a gallery, a short narrative, and the next project.

| # | Section | What it does | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Navbar | Work · Studio · Contact, plus News if they publish | Often a wordmark and a menu |
| 2 | Hero ★ | One signature project image or sequence, with a one-sentence statement of what they make and for whom | This is the site's signature moment |
| 3 | Disciplines *(optional)* | Filters or links: Architecture · Interiors · Landscape | Only if they truly span disciplines |
| 4 | Selected work ★ | 4–8 curated projects, large images, image + title + one line each, opening project pages | Curated, not complete; the full index is a page |
| 5 | Studio line | One to three lines on approach, one photo of the people or the space | Not a biography |
| 6 | Recognition *(optional)* | Awards, press, clients, teaching | Only if real |
| 7 | News / journal *(optional)* | Three latest items | Only if they publish regularly |
| 8 | Contact CTA | One sentence, email, phone, city | |
| 9 | Footer | Address(es), socials, legal | |

**Sub-pages (default):** Work (full index, filterable) · Project (template: hero image, title + facts table with location, year, client, size, status; concept statement under 100 words; drawings or process; gallery; short narrative; next project) · Studio / About (people, approach) · Contact · Privacy. Journal only if they write.

**Moments that fit:** scroll-scrubbed image or render sequence in the hero; image reveals on scroll; project hover with cursor label or image trail; page transitions between index and project; near-black or paper-white backgrounds with grain, sometimes a typographic watermark.

## 5. Generic (fallback): companies, agencies, organisations

Anything not above. Goal: contact.

What real services companies do (Instrument, thoughtbot): a hero statement; featured work or a case study right after; services; client logos as social proof; recognition or a report; culture or purpose; resources or news; a final CTA ("Start a project", "Let's talk"); footer.

| # | Section | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Navbar | Work · Services · About · Contact (+ Resources) + CTA |
| 2 | Hero ★ | Who it is for, what they get, one action |
| 3 | Featured work or proof | One case or 3–4 projects; or logos + a number |
| 4 | Services | 3–4 offers, one line each, links |
| 5 | Why / how ★ | Differentiators or process, one visual |
| 6 | Proof | Testimonial(s) or case results |
| 7 | About teaser | Two lines + photo, link to About |
| 8 | Resources / news *(optional)* | Only if they publish |
| 9 | Final CTA | One sentence, the action |
| 10 | Footer | Contact, legal |

**Sub-pages (default):** Services / Offer · Work / Cases · About · Contact · Privacy. Blog only if they publish.

**Moments that fit:** whatever the interview reveals; default to a typographic hero reveal plus one scroll moment in slot 5 and a quiet background.

## Choosing the anatomy

| The client... | Anatomy |
| --- | --- |
| sells software people log into | SaaS |
| does a trade or service at a location or at your home | Local business |
| sells one thing you can hold or download | Product |
| is hired for the quality of their past work | Studio / portfolio |
| none of the above, or a mix | Generic, then borrow sections from the closest one |

A mix is normal (a carpenter who also sells one chair: Local business with the Product "feature chapter" slot borrowed). Borrow at most two sections; if more are needed, the site type is wrong.
